ABSTRACT
This study investigates the application of machine learning and explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) to SME loan repayment-risk prediction in Nigeria, with emphasis on financial information that can be available outside conventional audited financial statements. The study used the supplied dataset of 4,612 loan records and seven predictor variables: current physical capital, current stock, current stock finished, revenue, expenses, profits and loan amount. The dependent variable was Loan_paid, coded 0 for not paid and 1 for paid. The analysis followed a quantitative, predictive research design and used data cleaning, exploratory analysis, stratified train-test splitting, class-weighted machine learning, cross-model comparison and SHAP-based explanation. Because the not-paid class is the economically important risk class, model evaluation emphasized its precision, recall and F1-score in addition to ROC-AUC. A class-weighted Random Forest was selected as the final risk model because, on the held-out test set, it achieved an accuracy of 0.626, precision of 0.72, recall of 0.72, F1-score of 0.72, and ROC-AUC of 0.614 when the not-paid outcome was treated as the risk class. SHAP analysis identified loan amount as the strongest global contributor, followed by current stock, current stock finished, profits, revenue, and expenses variables. The findings indicate that machine learning can support SME credit-risk screening, but the predictive performance of the present dataset is moderate rather than deployment-ready. The study therefore recommends combining these financial variables with richer informal financial signals such as transaction histories, cooperative repayment records, POS/mobile-money activity and verified business cash-flow histories, while retaining human oversight and explainability in credit decisions.
